# INV006: No blame for someone who hurts people?

> Published on Nov 25, 2026

The first proposition says no blame. When something goes wrong, look for causes, not someone to blame. It sounds kind, until someone gets hurt. What about the person who hurt them?

## 1. The objection

Someone hits a child. Someone cheats an old man out of his home. Someone burns a forest. "Look for causes" can sound like a shrug, like letting it happen again.

This is where people push hardest, and they should.

## 2. The other half of no credit

INV003 said blame and credit come as a pair. What you did well grew from others: a language, food, skills, tools, luck.

Harm grows the same way. The person who hurts others also came from somewhere: what was done to them, what they lacked, what they were taught, fear, illness, one bad moment.

That doesn't make the harm smaller. It makes it explainable. And what can be explained can often be prevented.

## 3. No blame isn't doing nothing

The asks still hold. Don't harm. Don't block others, *unless they harm or deceive someone.* So when someone harms, people can step in.

First, stop the harm. Then care for whoever was hurt. Then look for causes, and fix them.

Blame asks: who's guilty, and how should they suffer? No blame asks: what happened, how do we stop it, and how do we keep it from happening again?

## 4. Why blame works badly

Blame feels like justice. But it often makes things worse.

- **People hide.** When owning up gets you punished, people cover up. Aviation learned this long ago: pilots who report their own mistakes aren't punished for them, so mistakes get reported and fixed before they kill anyone.
- **The search stops.** Once someone is blamed, people stop asking why. Whatever made the harm possible stays in place.
- **Harm grows from harm.** Punishment adds more of it.

## 5. Safety, not punishment

What if someone keeps hurting people? Then others may need to keep away from them, or keep them away from others, for as long as the danger lasts. Not to make them suffer. Only to keep people safe, with the door open for them to come back.

This sits uneasily with the ground rule, anyone can walk away. Keeping someone from others is close to trapping them. Where that line goes is an open question. Better ideas are welcome.

The ground rule also protects whoever was hurt. Nobody is bound to stay near someone who hurts them.

## 6. Your feelings are yours

Anger at someone who hurt you, or someone you love, is natural. No blame doesn't ask you to stop feeling it. It's about what we do next.

You don't have to forgive. Nothing is owed back, not even that.

## 7. Owning up gets easier

Without blame, it's easier to say "I did that. I'm sorry. How can I help fix it?" Blame looks back for someone to punish. Responsibility looks forward to what needs fixing.

## In short

No blame doesn't mean no response. Stop the harm, care for the hurt, look for causes, fix them. Blame asks who should suffer. No blame asks how to keep it from happening again.
