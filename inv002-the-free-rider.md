# INV002: The free rider

> Published on Nov 24, 2026

It's the first question almost everyone asks. If nothing is owed back, and anyone can walk away, what about people who only take?

## 1. The worry

In this design, nobody has to give anything. So some people won't. They'll eat the bread, live in the homes, use the tools, and never help. Why would anyone else keep going?

It's a fair worry. It deserves a straight answer, not a slogan.

## 2. Most people give anyway

Look around. People already give without being paid or forced. Parents raise children. Neighbours shovel each other's snow. Volunteers wrote most of Wikipedia. Much of the software the internet runs on was built for free and shared.

People give because it feels good, because they care, because they're curious, or because something needed doing and they were there. Not everyone, not all the time. But enough.

## 3. Taking is cheap when there's enough

In the old world, a free rider really hurt. If there wasn't enough to go around, every taker meant someone else went without.

When everyone has enough, that changes. A song copied costs nothing. A spare loaf feeds someone instead of going stale. When machines do most of the hard work and the rest is shared thinly, a few people who only take don't break anything.

## 4. Rest is not free riding

Many people who look like takers aren't. They're ill, grieving, very young, very old, tired, or still finding what they love.

And all of us start and end our lives as free riders. Nobody paid back the people who fed them as a baby.

## 5. When it really breaks

It can break. If too few people give, the work that truly needs doing won't get done.

When that happens, the answer is never punishment. Remember: no blame, look for causes. So we ask:

- **Is the work too unpleasant?** If nobody would ever choose a job, that's a design problem. Make it better, or let machines do it.
- **Can people see what's needed?** Often people don't help because they don't know.
- **Is giving easy?** The easier and more joyful it is to help, the more people will.

How many takers a world can carry, nobody knows yet. That's an open question.

## In short

Some people will only take. That's fine, as long as enough people give. Our job isn't to chase the takers. It's to make giving easy, visible and joyful.
