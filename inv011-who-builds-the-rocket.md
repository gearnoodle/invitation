# INV011: Who builds the rocket?

If nothing is owed back and anyone can walk away, how do thousands of people stick with one project for decades?

## The worry

The International Space Station, the Large Hadron Collider, the Panama Canal. Huge things take huge numbers of people, for a very long time. In the old world, money and contracts held them together. Take those away, and why would anyone stay?

## Why people stay

- **Because it's thrilling.** Most people who build rockets would build them for free. Many already nearly do.
- **Because it matters.** A project everyone can see is worth doing pulls people in.
- **Because it's shared.** Nobody owns the rocket, so everybody can be proud of it.
- **Because it's in pieces.** Nobody has to give forty years. Thousands give a year or two each, and the project keeps going like a relay.

And when people do walk away, that's a signal. Maybe the project lost its way. Maybe it's time to change it.

## Big projects ahead

Not Dyson spheres. Things our grandchildren might actually see:

- **Clean energy for everyone,** from fusion or a worldwide solar and wind grid.
- **Pulling carbon out of the air,** to repair the climate.
- **Protecting coastal cities** as the seas rise.
- **A base on the Moon,** and perhaps the first steps on Mars.
- **Planetary defence,** spotting and nudging away dangerous asteroids.

The biggest project of all, enough for everybody, has its own post: INV010.

## In short

Rockets get built because people love building them. Many hands, a relay of years, nothing owed back.
