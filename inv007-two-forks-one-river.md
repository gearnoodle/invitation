# INV007: Two forks, one river

> Published on Nov 25, 2026

The right to fork says anyone can take this design and make their own. INV005 said that at best the designs still work together, but it's okay if they don't. Yet there's only one planet. What happens when two forks need the same river?

## 1. The problem

A design can split. The Earth can't. Two groups can live by different designs and still share one river, one forest, one coast, one sky.

Upstream, one fork wants a dam. Downstream, another needs the water to keep flowing. Forking an idea is easy. Forking a river isn't.

## 2. The river belongs to no one

The river isn't owned by whoever lives upstream, or by either design. It's shared by everyone it touches: both forks, the fish and the birds, and the people who come after.

## 3. The asks cross the line

A fork can change anything, even the three asks. But the people downstream didn't fork with them.

Don't harm: not people, not animals, not the Earth we share. A fork that poisons the river harms everyone below it, whatever design they live by.

## 4. Decide with everyone it touches

*Decide close to home* means most decisions are made by the people they affect. For a river, that's everyone along it, on both sides of the fork.

- **Everything is visible.** How much each side takes, and what goes back in.
- **Try small first.** A small change before a big one, and easy to undo.

## 5. Enough makes sharing easier

People have fought over water and land for as long as we know. Most of those fights come from not having enough, or being afraid of not having enough.

When both forks have enough, there's less to fight over. A river is easier to share when nobody fears going thirsty.

## 6. When it still doesn't fit

Sometimes two forks want things that can't both happen. One wants the valley flooded for power, the other wants it as it is.

No design makes that go away. What helps is the rest of this one: talk in the open, look for causes instead of blame, and look for a third way, like power from the sun instead of the dam. And anyone, from either fork, can still walk away.

How to decide when wants truly clash is an open question. Better ideas are welcome.

## 7. Two experiments, side by side

Two forks sharing a river are also two experiments. If one finds a better way to share water, the other can borrow it. Nothing is owed back for that either.

## In short

Designs can split. The planet can't. Whatever design you live by, the river belongs to no one, decisions belong to everyone it touches, and the three asks still cross the line.
