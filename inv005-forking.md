# INV005: Forking

> Published on Nov 25, 2026

At the bottom of this design sit three rules. Rule 0, the ground rule: **anyone can walk away.** Rule 1, the evolution: **everything can be changed when there is great need.** And rule 2, the right to fork, stuck right into the evolution layer: **anyone can fork this design.**

The evolution lets this design change. But what if you want something it will never become? Then you can fork it.

## 1. What forking means

The word comes from software. Anyone can copy shared code, change what they want, and take it in a new direction. The old version keeps going, and so does the new one. One road, two paths.

This design works the same way. Take it, change it, and start your own.

## 2. Walking away together

The ground rule says anyone can walk away. Walking away alone is hard. Forking lets you walk away together, and toward something.

So it's okay to fork this design and gather supporters for the new one. Anyone who wants to go there can.

## 3. Nothing is owed

You don't need permission, and you don't owe this design anything. Nothing is owed back. You don't even need to say where your version came from, though it's kind to.

## 4. Compatible at best

In the best case, the designs still work together. People can move between them, share what works, and borrow each other's ideas. Like two dialects of one language.

## 5. Okay even if not

Sometimes the designs won't fit together. That's okay too. Different people want different lives.

What still matters is how we treat each other. Don't harm, don't deceive, don't block others. That includes the people who forked.

## 6. Won't everything split apart?

It could. But leaving is never free. You leave people, places and habits behind, so most people stay where things work.

And when many people leave, that tells us something. It's a great need, the kind the evolution is for. A fork can show what's missing, and the good ideas can come back.

## In short

Anyone can walk away, and anyone can take this design with them and make it their own. Better a design that splits than one that traps.
