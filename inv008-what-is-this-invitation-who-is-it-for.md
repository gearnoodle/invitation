# INV008: What is this invitation? Who is it for?

*Exploration notes, not a draft yet.*

## What is this invitation?

- Not a plan, not a manifesto, not a movement to join. Nobody has to follow it.
- An open door: "Come in, look around, leave whenever you like."
- A design in progress, which anyone can correct, extend or fork.
- An invitation *towards* something: one day, everyone finally has enough.
- Maybe closer to a question than an answer: what if we designed how we live together on purpose?

**This is only the design phase.** Nothing here is built yet. Once people start living it, everything can turn out differently from how it started, and that's part of the plan.

## Who is it for?

Possible answers to explore:

- **Everyone.** It's about all of humanity, so nobody is left outside.
- **Anyone who feels something is off.** People who sense we already know how to give everyone enough, and wonder why we don't.
- **The tired.** People worn out by keeping score, competing and proving themselves.
- **The builders and tinkerers.** People who like to design systems, write code, plant gardens or fix benches.
- **The sceptics.** People who think it can't work. Their objections make the design better.
- **Generations to come.** People who aren't born yet, who'll live inside whatever we design.
- **Even those who walk away.** The invitation stays open.

## What does saying yes look like?

There's no form, no membership, no oath. Saying yes might look like:

- **A thought.** Noticing when you keep score, and letting it go once. Circle zero.
- **A small act.** Giving something and truly expecting nothing back.
- **A habit.** Thanking the thing, not ranking the person. Looking for causes instead of blame.
- **A conversation.** Telling someone about the idea, or arguing with it.
- **A contribution.** Adding an idea, a correction or a post to this blog.
- **A fork.** Taking the design somewhere new.
- **Walking away.** Even that is allowed. Maybe it's a yes for later.

Saying yes is probably not one moment but many small ones, in whichever circle you can reach.

How would anyone know you've said yes? They don't need to. A yes isn't a badge, and nothing is owed back, not even a sign that you've joined.

## Do you need to agree with the ten points?

Probably not. An invitation that demands agreement first is a contract, not an invitation.

- **Agreement isn't the entry ticket.** You can come in curious, doubtful, or just tired of how things are.
- **Disagreement is part of the design.** Mistakes welcome says even the ten points could be wrong. A disagreement is a correction waiting to happen.
- **You can take one point and leave nine.** Someone might only like "no blame" and practise it at work. That still counts.
- **The right to fork covers the rest.** If you disagree deeply, take the design and make your own.
- **The three asks are the closest thing to a line.** Don't harm, don't deceive, don't block others. Not as a test to get in, but as what lets everyone share the space.

Is there anything you'd need to agree with? Perhaps only that everyone should one day have enough. And even that is open to argument.

## Who might not feel invited?

- People who benefit from the current design and fear losing status or power.
- People who are too busy surviving to think about design at all.
- People who distrust anything that sounds utopian.

How could it reach them too? Not by persuading, but by showing. Small places where enough already exists: a shared garden, a gift that stays a gift. People busy surviving need the floor first, not an argument. And people who fear losing status may find relief in no longer having to win.

## Two last questions

1. **Can an invitation be for everyone without becoming for no one?** Yes, because it isn't aimed at a target group. It's one open door with many ways in. Each person enters through the circle they can reach: a thought, a friend, a street, a system.
2. **Which circle does this post sit in?** Circle zero, the inner world. Every yes starts there, in how we think about giving, owing and enough.
