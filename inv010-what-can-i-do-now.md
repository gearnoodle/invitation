# INV010: What can I do now?

The biggest project humanity could take on isn't a rocket or a reactor. It's enough for everybody. So where do you start?

## Why this invitation exists

This is the point of the whole invitation: not to hand out orders, but to offer a clear frame of mind. A simple way to see what's worth doing, and where to start. Once that's clear, everyone can find their own part in the biggest project of all.

That's why it matters that more people help design it. A frame of mind drawn by one person fits one person. Drawn by many, it can fit almost everyone.

And it takes patience. This is work for generations, not a quick fix. We start it so that others can carry it on.

## The biggest project of all

Enough for everybody. Food, water, a home and care for all eight billion people, and every person who comes after.

It's bigger than any rocket. It has no launch date and no finish line. And unlike a rocket, everyone can help build it, starting with circle zero, and the person next to you.

## "It'll never happen"

The sceptic's answer to all this deserves a fair hearing.

### The objections

1. **"People are selfish."** Without money or pressure, nobody will do the hard work. (More on this in INV002.)
2. **"The comfortable won't move."** People who have enough have settled down. Why would they change anything?
3. **"The powerful won't let it."** Those at the top will defend what they have, with money, laws and force if needed.
4. **"It's been tried."** Communes and revolutions promised a fair world. Many ended in poverty, or worse.
5. **"Who pays?"** Everything here runs on money. A floor for eight billion people costs a fortune.
6. **"We live in capitalism."** It's how the world works. You can't just step outside it.

All fair. Some of them may even be right, for now.

### What the sceptic gets right

It won't happen all at once. It won't happen by persuading everyone. And nobody should trust a design that promises paradise by Tuesday.

This isn't a short project. We probably won't see it finished in our lifetime, maybe not even in two. Like planting trees whose shade we'll never sit in. That's the attitude for this whole invitation: patient, generous, and long.

### What the sceptic misses

Pieces of it already exist, right inside capitalism:

- **Libraries.** Take a book, owe nothing but its return.
- **Public healthcare.** In many countries, care doesn't depend on what you earn.
- **Wikipedia and open-source software.** Built mostly by people who were never paid for it.
- **Cooperatives.** Mondragon in Spain is a large group of businesses owned and run by their workers.
- **Basic income trials.** Finland, Kenya and others have tested giving people money with no strings.

None of these is the whole floor. But each one is a piece that works.

### How it could actually happen

- **Grow what already works.** More libraries of things, more shared care, more open knowledge.
- **Let machines make things cheaper.** The cheaper food, energy and homes get, the easier the floor becomes.
- **Shift what feels normal.** The weekend, the vote for women and the end of child labour all once sounded impossible.
- **Never force it.** Anyone can walk away. A design that has to be imposed has already failed.

The sceptic is right that it won't happen overnight. But "never" is a long time, and pieces of the floor are already under our feet.

## Through the circles

The circles from INV000 have no order and no climbing. But for most of us, the floor naturally starts close and widens:

- **Yourself.** If you have nothing left to give, take care of yourself. That's not selfish. It's a brick in the floor too.
- **The people close to you.** A meal shared, a hand lent, a friend who isn't alone.
- **Your street and town.** A shared garden, a tool library, a bench.
- **The bigger circles.** Systems, the crowd, civilisation, the Earth.

Reach into whichever you can. Nobody has to go further than they're able. But if you have the time, skills and energy, you can help in the bigger circles and reach many people at once. Not because you owe it, but because you can.

## How to think about it

1. **It's mostly a sharing problem, not a making one.** The world already grows roughly enough food for everyone. What's missing is a design that gets it to everyone.
2. **Start small, and make it unconditional.** One town where nobody falls below the floor, whatever they do. Learn from it, then copy what works.
3. **Make it visible.** Show openly who has enough and who doesn't, so gaps get fixed, not hidden.
4. **Let machines take the dull work.** Fewer horrible jobs, fewer people stuck doing them.
5. **Measure by the floor, not the ceiling.** Ask how the worst-off person is doing, not what the average is.

## What can I do now?

- **Check your own floor.** Food, rest, a safe place. If something's missing, start there.
- **Share one spare thing.** A tool, a meal, a room for a night.
- **Notice who's below the floor near you.** Then ask, don't assume, what would help.
- **Join or start something local.** A food share, a tool library, a repair café.
- **Give an hour.** Once a week, once a month. Small and steady beats big and rare.
- **Talk about it.** Share this blog, argue with it, add a correction.

## In short

The floor gets built by many hands, a little at a time, nothing owed back. Start where you are.
