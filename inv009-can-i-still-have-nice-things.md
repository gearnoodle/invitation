# INV009: Can I still have nice things?

*And other silly questions, welcome.*

> "The biggest shift might be this. We used to ask: how can I make my life better, and maybe the lives of the people closest to me? Now the question widens: **how can I make everybody's life better, equally, including my own?** Not me first, not me last. Me too, and equally everybody else."

Newcomers ask the best questions. Here are a few, answered lightly. None of this is a rule. It's a way of thinking you're invited to try on, like a jumper. Keep it if it fits.

## How does someone in this world think?

Before the silly questions, a guess at the mind behind them. Imagine someone who has always had enough. What would they care about, and what would they shrug at?

**What matters:**

- **People.** Time with friends, family, a neighbour on a bench.
- **Health and rest.** A body that feels good, and sleep without worry.
- **Curiosity.** Learning, making, wondering, just because it's interesting.
- **Usefulness.** Helping where help is needed, because it feels good, not because it's owed.
- **The Earth.** Clean air, clean water, a living world to hand on.

**What fades:**

- **Being above others.** No ranks to climb, so no one to look down on.
- **Owning for show.** Things are for using and enjoying, not proving.
- **Keeping score.** Who gave what stops being interesting.
- **Fear of falling.** With a floor beneath you, there's nothing to fall through.

### Feelings

Envy, jealousy and shame often grow from comparison. When nobody is ranked, they have less to feed on. They don't vanish. They just get quieter, and easier to look at.

Other feelings get more room. Gratitude without the itch to repay. Calm without the fear of losing everything. Sadness, too, because with less hurry there's time to feel it properly.

Feelings aren't good or bad here. They're weather. You notice them, name them, and let them pass.

### Behaviour

When nothing is owed back, behaviour shifts in small ways. People offer help without waiting to be asked. They say no without long excuses. They ask for help without feeling smaller.

Kindness stops being a strategy. You're not kind to be liked, or to collect a favour. You're kind because it's the easiest way to live next to someone.

And when someone behaves badly, the first question isn't "whose fault?" but "what was missing?"

### Thinking and pondering

Slow thinking is a luxury most of us can't afford. Rent, work and worry crowd it out. With enough, there's space for it again.

The questions change too. Less "how do I get ahead?", more "what is this for?", "what do I actually enjoy?" and "what would be good for everyone, including people not born yet?"

The biggest shift might be this. We used to ask: how can I make my life better, and maybe the lives of the people closest to me? Now the question widens: how can I make everybody's life better, equally, including my own? Not me first, not me last. Me too, and equally everybody else.

*Or, put another way: life is one big cake. Everyone gets a slice, nobody gets the whole thing, and yes, you're allowed a slice too.*

Pondering isn't wasted time. Sitting under a tree, wondering about the universe in circle nine, might be one of the most human things there is.

Nobody becomes this person overnight. It's a direction, not a test.

## Can I still have nice things?

Yes. The floor is enough. The ceiling is open. Good coffee, a holiday by the sea, a ridiculously shiny bike: all above the floor, all yours to enjoy.

The invitation is just to ask, now and then: do I want this, or do I want to be seen having it? Both answers are allowed. Only one of them needs a mirror.

## What about a Bentley? A penthouse? A private jet?

Ah, the big ones. Here the ceiling is still open, but the three asks come along for the ride.

- **A Bentley.** Lovely to look at. In an enough world, it might be a shared car you book for a weekend, or one made by people who simply love building beautiful things. Nobody needs to own it to enjoy it.
- **A penthouse.** A view is wonderful. But one family in forty rooms while others have none breaks the floor. Maybe the penthouse becomes the place everyone in the building takes turns watching the sunset from.
- **A private jet.** Not a no. A jet burns a lot of fuel, so it asks for a good reason. Once in a long while, say once in ten years, a flight could go ahead if it's described openly: how it will be used, and what for. Then the people it affects decide together. If enough of them say yes, off it goes. Still, it's worth a quiet look inward. Wanting a jet of your own, just to have it, often says the old way of thinking is still at the controls: being above, being apart. No blame, it's how most of us were raised. But it may be a sign this direction isn't quite yours yet.

The trick is noticing what these things were really for. Comfort? Beauty? Speed? Or being above others? The first three are welcome. The last one doesn't have much to stand on here.

*All of this is a design point for now, not a final answer. Luxury in an enough world needs more thinking, and better ideas are welcome.*

## Do I have to give away my stuff?

No. And nobody comes to collect your sofa. In fact, a home on the floor already comes with one: a bed, a table, a sofa, the basics. If you really want a better one, swap it. Giving is an invitation, not a tax.

But you might notice something. A lent drill comes back with a story. A shared ladder means your neighbour finally waves. Things get lighter when they move around.

## Is my cat on the floor too?

Yes. Every person, every animal, and the Earth itself. Your cat will not be informed. Your cat already assumes it owns the floor, the sofa and you.

## Can I walk away from my own family dinner?

Technically, yes. Anyone can walk away. But there's dessert coming, so why would you?

The ground rule isn't about leaving at the first sigh. It's about knowing you could, so staying becomes a choice rather than a trap. Strangely, that often makes dinner nicer.

## What if I really like being thanked?

Wonderful. Thanks stays. It's the praise that we gently turn towards the thing. "Lovely soup" rather than "you're the best cook alive". Your soup gets the glory. You get the joy, and the leftovers.

## What if I still keep score in my head?

Everyone does. Most of us grew up with a little accountant living behind our eyes. You don't have to fire them. Just give them a day off now and then and see what happens.

Catching yourself keeping score isn't failing. It's circle zero, working.

## Who checks if I'm doing it right?

Nobody. If someone starts checking, that's the bit worth fixing. (More on saying yes in INV008.)

## What if I think this is all a bit silly?

Then you're in excellent company. Tell us why. (INV008 says more about sceptics.)

## In short

This is a way of thinking, offered, not enforced. Enjoy your nice things. Pet your cat. Stay for dessert. Try a point or two, and leave the rest on the table for another day.
